The language

Read music without decoding it.

Instead of note names, the app shows you the structure of the music: colours and shapes.

Seven colours, and you choose what they say.

ChromaKeys, the app’s colour piano, paints your handpan. The colour tells you either the note itself or its place in the scale.

The colour says…
Notes are written…

The first degree is always red, whatever the key, and the chord’s shape doesn’t move: it’s like a capo on a guitar.

Each note keeps its chakra colour, whatever the scale: Do is always red, Sol sky blue. A chord then becomes a mix of colours, one per note.

On top of the colours, the app joins up the notes of a chord and draws a glowing figure, a chord constellation. There are three ways to trace it.

Why this app

“The tool I wish I’d been given as a child”

I started the drums at 4, and music theory put me off playing, so I learned by ear. At 12, at the piano, I realised my memory wasn’t built to decode abstract notes: it’s visual-motor and spatial. With the handpan I hit the same wall: spaceships with no pilot’s manual. So I drew. From those drawings came the tool I wish I’d been given: music theory made visible, in languages a child can understand.

Questions

What people ask me.

Which device do I need?

A computer or tablet, in your browser. A phone screen is too small to show your handpan and its chords properly. On iPhone and iPad, Apple doesn’t let browsers pick up an instrument connected by USB: to connect a digital pan, open the app in Web MIDI Browser, free on the App Store.

Do I need a digital pan?

No. The app is designed first for your acoustic handpan, whatever it is. If you have a digital pan, you can connect that too.

What’s actually free?

Everything you need to get to know your instrument and play: your scale, your chords, the repertoire, singing to find your voice, playing technique, and saving what you create.

What if I don’t know my scale?

Play it into the mic and the app finds it.

I’ve never learned to read music. Is it for me?

Yes. You start with colours and emotions. Note names and theory come when you ask for them.

Will I be asked for my card details?

No. The free account doesn’t ask for a card, and it stays free with no time limit. You’ll only be asked for your card details when you choose a paid plan.

What if I want to go further one day?

There are two paid plans in the app. Essential, €3.49 a month, transposes a song that doesn’t fit your scale and lets you play along to the catalogue’s grooves, with a drummer. Studio, €9.99 a month, adds singing exercises. First month free on monthly plans, and you can change plan whenever you like.

How do I start?

Sign up, put your handpan in front of you and let the app listen to it. It takes care of the rest.
