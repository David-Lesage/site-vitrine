This article is about the **Neotone electronic handpan**. It comes in two models: the **Neotone¹**, with 10 notes, and the **Neotone¹ Mutant**, with 19 notes. Neither is "better": each one answers a different project.

The Mutant also has a **small built-in screen** on the instrument. The 10-note Neotone¹ does not.

## Choose the 19 notes if…

### You want to make it your main instrument

With 19 notes, you're no longer locked into a single colour of music. You can move from one piece to another, from one mood to another, without changing instruments.

### You want to accompany your voice on well-known songs

To accompany a song, you need its <abbr title="At least three notes played together, for example C, E, G.">chords</abbr>. As David puts it: "a 19-note is better, because it lets you play all the chords (degrees) of the major and minor keys on a single scale."

Each of these chords has its place in the <abbr title="The home note that a piece and its chords revolve around, for example &quot;in D&quot;.">key</abbr>: these are the **<abbr title="The position of a chord in the key, numbered from I to VII.">scale degrees</abbr>**, from I to VII. The Mutant has **all seven**. The 10-note Neotone¹ does not have all seven degrees.

And these chords keep the same shape when you change key: that's the story told in [The handpan capo](/en/blog/le-capo-du-handpan).

### You want bass under your voice

Low notes set beneath your singing give a piece its grounding. The [vocal layouts](/en/blog/layouts-vocaux-handpan) show how to use them.

## Choose the 10 notes if…

### You want to complete your acoustic handpan

You already have an acoustic handpan and you love it. It just lacks **bass** or **a few specific notes** for certain pieces. The 10-note Neotone¹ can bring them, set down right beside it.

You keep your acoustic at the centre; the electronic one plays what the other can't. The article [Completing your acoustic handpan with a Neotone](/en/blog/completer-son-handpan-acoustique-avec-un-neotone) details this case.

The Mutant can do the same thing. It becomes interesting if you sing while playing: its extra notes leave you **room for the melody**, above the chord. The same article compares the two.

### You want to discover the handpan

Ten notes make simple ground for your first steps. You hear music that makes sense right away.

### You want to improvise or meditate within one scale

A single <abbr title="A set of chosen notes that sound good together.">scale</abbr>, under your fingers, without searching for anything. This is the most natural use of the handpan.

### You want a lighter and more accessible instrument

Fewer notes, no screen: the 10-note model is the simplest and cheapest. Up-to-date prices are in the [calculator](/en/le-neotone#calculateur).

The simplest thing is to play both. Come and try them at the [showroom in Paris](/en/showroom): your hands will quickly tell you which one speaks to you. And to compare prices for your country, the [calculator](/en/le-neotone#calculateur) will do the math for you.
