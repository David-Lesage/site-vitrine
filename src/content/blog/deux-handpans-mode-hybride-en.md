Many players end up owning **two handpans** — and playing them as two separate instruments. This article is mostly about the **acoustic duo**: two acoustic handpans. The hybrid electro-acoustic duo (one acoustic and one Neotone) and the electronic duo (two Neotones) are explained in [Playing two handpans together](/en/blog/deux-handpans-ensemble/). [Handpan Constellation Studio](/en/handpan-app) looks at them differently: it lays both shells side by side and draws each chord **across the two of them**, with the play order and the hands. They're no longer two pans. They're one instrument.

## There's always a note missing

A handpan scale is a choice, and therefore a renunciation. You have nine or ten notes, beautiful ones, and there will always be **that one** missing note that would make the chord you dream of playable. It's the most common frustration, and the most poorly explained: you think you can't do it, when the note simply doesn't exist under your hands.

The "**Complete your scale**" funnel (*Compléter ta gamme*) answers that honestly. It compares your instrument's real notes — octaves included — with what the chords require, and shows you, **chord by chord**, which notes are missing.

## What a second pan unlocks, in plain words

The real progress isn't the list of missing notes: it's the translation into music. Each suggestion now tells you **how many pieces it makes playable**, and which ones. Hallelujah, Let It Be, No Woman No Cry… rather than abstract degrees.

The app also points out what you already have without knowing it: the **relative scale**, often playable as-is on your pan, which it would be a shame to overlook before having anything made.

One word of honesty: those counters rest on a still-modest catalogue of scores. They tell the truth, but not yet the *whole* truth — the repertoire will grow as scores are added to the [music library](/en/blog/bibliotheque-musicale-handpan).

## 🌗 The second pan, and the acoustic duo banner

> **Updated 23 October 2026.** The two checkboxes in the right-hand panel no longer exist. Your instruments now live in **My handpans**, as cards, and **🤝 My duo** has two slots. As soon as your two handpans are set down, the app asks **what you want to do with them**: 🌈 play everything, 🎵 play a song, 🎯 complete your key, or ✋ keep as is. Under the pans, two rows of cards: the 7 chords of your key, then the **extra** chords that only your two handpans together allow. A "✕ Un seul handpan" (*one handpan only*) button takes you back to a single instrument. The whole flow is explained in [Playing two handpans together](/en/blog/deux-handpans-ensemble/).

A banner appears above your pans. In its September version, the one in the screenshot below: **🌗 Your 2 handpans: *the first* + *the second***, with a **⇄ swap** to exchange their places, the current tonal frame, and the count that says everything in one line:

- how many chords the **first pan** plays alone;
- how many the **second** plays alone;
- how many you have **with both**.

That last number is the real subject. For those chords, the voicing is genuinely computed across the two instruments — lowest root in the bass — and the path crosses the gap from one pan to the other. You see at a glance which hand goes where, and the play order follows.

A word on vocabulary, because the app now draws the line: two acoustics side by side is an **acoustic duo**. An acoustic **and** a Neotone is a **hybrid electro-acoustic duo** — same banner, different rules; that is the subject of [completing your acoustic handpan with a Neotone](/en/blog/completer-son-handpan-acoustique-avec-un-neotone). The app never mixes the two families by default: it *offers* you the hybrid duo instead of refusing.

## Your repertoire, right from loading

Another quiet but decisive change: you no longer have to ask. As soon as an acoustic scale is loaded in Discover › Degrees & emotions (*Découvrir › Degrés & émotions*), a line appears under the 7 degrees: "🎵 ***N* pieces playable with your scale**", with a "see →" that opens the list. The information arrives before the question. And **which note would unlock the most** is answered by "Complete" and by the [✨ Reveal](/en/blog/reveler-les-accords-de-ton-handpan) screen.

## And if you want to hear before deciding

Two details are worth their weight in gold before ordering anything:

- **"The Star of the 24 keys"** (*L'Étoile des 24 tonalités*, the former key wheel) lets you listen to your scale transposed into another key; the same tool is also a shortcut in 🧰 Tools. You hear the future instrument before it exists.
- The **440 / 432 Hz tuning setting** tunes the app to *your* handpan, so what you hear on screen sounds right with what you hold in your hands.

Acoustic mode, "Complete" and "The Star of the 24 keys" are **free**, and your duo is saved to your account, even on the free tier: it follows you on all your devices. And if the second pan becomes a real desire, the app tells you exactly which notes to ask for; those notes work for an acoustic instrument just as well as for a [Neotone](/en/neotone).

Open [Handpan Constellation Studio](/en/handpan-app), load your scale and see what you're really missing. It's often less than you think. And to explore all this together, my [handpan lessons](/en/cours) are open.
