Until now, [Handpan Constellation Studio's](/en/handpan-app) visual score was there to **keep** and to **pass on**: you exported it as a [PDF](/en/blog/exporter-ses-partitions-handpan), printed it, sent it. It can now do one more thing, and it's the one that changes daily practice: it **plays itself**. The current diagram lights up, the page scrolls on its own, and all you have to do is follow.

## Reading a score already means doing two jobs

When you work through a chord chart, your brain does two jobs at once: playing, and **knowing where you are**. The second one eats almost all the energy. You lose your line, you repeat the wrong verse, you stop to check — and the musical momentum drops.

A player that points at the exact spot solves that quietly.

## ▶ The player: a score you can listen to

A **▶ / ⏸ / ⏹** bar plays the chords **in the order of the lyrics**. The current diagram lights up, the page scrolls by itself: impossible to get lost. You choose block chords or arpeggios, you set the BPM live, and the arpeggios really do unfold at the tempo — in eighths or quarters, depending on what you're working on.

It's also a way to learn a piece by ear: you listen to it turn at 40 BPM, very slowly, until the progression becomes obvious.

## ×N: saying how many times

Every chord carries a small **×N** badge. One click and you say how many times to play it, from 1 to 8. The player takes it into account, and the ×N values appear on the PDF — because a chart without repeats is only half a score.

In the same spirit, a song now carries its **time signature** — 4/4, 3/4, 6/8 or 2/4 — displayed near the title, on the preview as well as in the exports.

## 🎼 The music stand (*Pupitre*): full screen, screen awake

Here's the little detail that helps every single day. One **🎼** button and your score goes **full screen**, ready to play from an iPad or a phone propped in front of you: scroll with a finger, zoom by double-tap or pinch, and above all **the screen stays awake** while you play. No more screen going dark mid-chorus, no file to generate, no waiting.

## 🖨 And for those who love paper

A piece you composed now prints the way it's played: **one line per part**, with its colour band (Verse, Chorus, Bridge…), the diagrams in order, and all your settings respected — Eco-Ink mode, landscape, legend. An optional memo page lists the chords section by section, with their repeats.

You can also choose the diagram size — S, M, L or XL — for a score that reads from a distance, and reorder lyric lines by drag and drop.

## A score written by several hands

One last point, and it matters: shared scores keep track of **who contributed what**, first and last name shown. Placing a chord in the right spot, fixing a repeat, specifying a time signature: small gestures, but end to end they build a handpan repertoire that existed nowhere else. It's the same spirit as the [feedback feature](/en/blog/feedback-ameliorer-handpan-studio) — a tool we make together.

Exports made without Studio access carry a discreet "free version" watermark; everything else — player, music stand, repeats — stays under your fingers.
