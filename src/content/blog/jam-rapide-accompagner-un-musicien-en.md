[Handpan Constellation Studio's](/en/handpan-app) **Quick Jam** (⚡ *Jam Rapide* in the app, still labelled in French) turns a line thrown across the room — "we're in G minor, I-IV-V on the verse" — into a ready-to-play score, with named sections and diagrams in the right order. Three clicks. About as long as the guitarist needs to finish tuning.

## The scene every handpan player knows

A campfire, a living room, a jam. Someone says "it's in A minor, four chords, you following?". Everyone starts. You're still wondering whether your scale contains an E, which note to strike first, and whether what comes out will be in tune.

The problem isn't musical, it's **logistical**. You need fifteen seconds to translate what you've just been told into something your hands can read.

## Three clicks, one score

Quick Jam does exactly that translation. You give the key, you lay out the degrees per section — I-IV-V on the verse, III-IV-VI-V on the chorus — and the app hands you a full score: named sections, chord diagrams in order, ready to play or print.

The harmony comes out right across the four scale families: major, natural minor, harmonic minor and melodic minor — including the [three faces of minor](/en/blog/les-trois-mineurs-handpan).

## "I don't know the key, just the chords"

That's the most useful door, because it's the most common situation. People rarely dictate degrees; they dictate chord names.

Type them exactly as you're given them — "Am, F, C, G", or in Do Re Mi if that's your language — and the app **works out the key** on its own, in plain words. If a chord doesn't fit the key it found, it points that out gently rather than flattening it, and offers the credible alternatives. One click, and you have your scale, your piece and your score.

## The handpan you'd need

On the way out of Quick Jam, a "See the scale" card (*Voir la gamme*) draws the matching instrument on a **real Neotone shell**: each chord is voiced root · third · fifth · octave, exactly as in Logic Mode, and the shell picks itself — Neotone¹ with 10 notes if that's enough, Mutant with 19 otherwise.

So before playing a single note you can see whether the piece fits your instrument — and if you play a [Neotone](/en/le-neotone), you can load that layout straight away.

## Transposing for the voice

The last jam reflex: someone sings, and the key is too high. The **♭ / ♯** buttons shift the whole piece by a semitone, chords included, and "↩ Back to original" undoes everything. It's the move described in [Transposing to sing](/en/blog/transposer-pour-chanter-handpan), applied to the whole piece instead of a single note.

## And then?

The resulting score isn't a dead end: it opens full screen on the **music stand** (*Pupitre*), it prints, it stays in your pieces and it plays back chord by chord — that's the subject of [Your score comes alive](/en/blog/ta-partition-prend-vie).

Next time someone calls a key over their shoulder, open [Handpan Constellation Studio](/en/handpan-app) and make it playable before the first verse is over. And if you'd like to learn to hear those progressions instead of typing them, my [handpan lessons](/en/cours) start right there.
