Playing handpan by colours means learning to play without reading a single note of music theory: every sound on your instrument gets a colour (the **ChromaKeys**) and every chord draws a geometric shape (a **Constellation**). Your eye recognises the colour, your hand follows the shape, and the music happens — naturally, through gesture and emotion rather than theory. The method does not depend on the instrument: it reads the same way on an acoustic handpan and on an electronic one.

## Why colours instead of sheet music

Traditional notation asks you to translate an abstract symbol into a sound, then into a gesture. Three steps, three chances to lose your way. The ChromaKeys method removes one of them: the colour **is** the sound. A C is no longer a black dot on five lines — it's a red you spot instantly on your pan.

This approach leans on a simple fact about the brain: we memorise a colour and a shape far faster than a written code. That's why so many beginners who thought they were "not musical" start playing real pieces within a few sessions.

### Each note, a colour

The seven degrees of a scale get seven stable colours. Whatever the key of your handpan, the logic stays the same: the first degree always keeps its hue, the fifth its own, and so on. Learn it once, play it everywhere.

### Each chord, a shape

When several notes sound together, their colours connect into a **Constellation**: a triangle, a polygon, a recognisable figure. You no longer memorise a list of notes — you memorise a drawing. Replaying a chord becomes as simple as reproducing a shape.

## How to start in practice

1. **Map the colours on your pan.** Link each note to its ChromaKey until the connection becomes automatic.
2. **Play simple shapes.** Begin with two- or three-note Constellations: your ear confirms what your eye suggests.
3. **Let emotion lead.** Each colour carries an emotional tone (balance, momentum, nostalgia…). Choose the feeling first, and the note follows.

The easiest way in is to see those colours and shapes live on a screen, aligned with your instrument. That's exactly what the [Handpan Constellation Studio app](/en/handpan-app) does: it shows your pan in colour, draws Constellations in real time and guides you step by step — the Discovery version is free.

## And if you play an electronic handpan

The method shines even brighter on an [electronic Neotone handpan](/en/neotone): the instrument and the screen speak the same visual language, you switch scales with a single gesture, and you can play with headphones at any hour. Colours stay your guiding thread, whatever scale is loaded.

Want to be guided step by step? [David Lesage's lessons](/en/cours) teach the ChromaKeys method from scratch, at your own pace, until playing becomes second nature.

Learning handpan by colours isn't a trick: it's a way to make music finally **visible** — and therefore accessible to everyone.
