[Handpan Constellation Studio's](/en/handpan-app) melody editor (*Éditeur de mélodies* in the app, still labelled in French) gives you what was missing between your hands and your memory: your pan on one side, a **timeline** on the other. Click a note on the pan and it lands on the time line. You listen, you move things around, you loop, and you export as `.mid`. A melody that existed tonight still exists tomorrow morning.

The editor shows your current scale **whether your handpan is acoustic or electronic** — live MIDI capture needs an electronic instrument, and an acoustic one can record itself through the microphone, as described below.

## On the handpan, a melody doesn't get written down

Chords, you end up keeping: they have a shape, a colour, a [diagram](/en/blog/diagrammes-accords-handpan-pdf). A melody doesn't. You play it, it passes, it's gone. And the classical staff doesn't help much: it asks you to read music, and above all it says nothing about **where on the pan** to put your hand.

So you keep replaying the same three phrases — the ones your fingers memorised on their own.

## Your pan on the left, your timeline in the centre

The idea fits in one image. On the left, your handpan as you know it, each note wearing its **ChromaKey**. In the centre, a horizontal timeline with its bars. Click a note and a coloured block appears at the cursor.

From there everything is hands-on:

- **Move** a block to change when it sounds;
- **Stretch** it to lengthen the note;
- **Delete** it with a right-click;
- All of it **snapped** to the grid of your choice — quarter, eighth, sixteenth or triplet — in 4/4, 3/4 or 6/8.

A **pencil** tool lets you draw straight into the grid, with eraser and undo. And two colour logics live side by side: **ChromaKeys** (colour follows the note) or **degree** (colour follows the harmonic role, as in [Logic Mode](/en/blog/handpan-emotions-degres)). The same melody, told two ways.

## Listen, loop, speed up

The playhead lights the notes back up on the pan as it travels: you watch your melody being played on the instrument, not just on a grid. Tempo is set in BPM, the **loop** runs over a bar or a selection, and progressive speed-up adds 5 BPM every four passes — the old recipe for getting a phrase into your fingers.

A pulse bar completes the picture: the beats light up, the first one in orange, and the **left (blue)** and **right (red)** feet alternate at the tempo. You stop counting, you start swaying.

## Recording what you play

Live MIDI capture applies to electronic handpans only: it needs a MIDI output, which an acoustic pan does not have.

If your [Neotone](/en/neotone) is connected over MIDI, you can also work the other way round: press ⏺, a count-in starts, and **what you play is written into the grid**. Then you fix with the mouse whatever your hand missed.

**On an acoustic handpan**, the microphone does the listening. Since 22 October, Import offers *⏺ M'enregistrer maintenant* (record myself now): you play, *⏹ Arrêter* (stop), and the app suggests the notes; you correct them by ear. The sound never leaves your device. It's also a tool of its own in 🧰 Tools: *Transcrire mon jeu au micro* (transcribe my playing by microphone). It is still young: reliable on slow, clean playing, much less on fast playing.

Either way the MIDI bridge stays open: import a `.mid` file, export yours, take your melody into any music software.

## Where to find it

The editor opens from **Create** (*Créer*) and from the **music library** (*Bibliothèque musicale*), and it loads your current scale — or any scale from the Atlas, transposable. The melody editor will be part of the **Studio** version; during the beta, it is open to everyone.

Your next melody deserves better than your memory tomorrow morning. Open [Handpan Constellation Studio](/en/handpan-app), drop three notes, and watch them loop. And if you'd like to be guided while composing, my [handpan lessons](/en/cours) are made for that.
