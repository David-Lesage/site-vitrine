**Subject:** Re: ask for official steel pictures for my Yishama webpage on my website

Hi Altin,

Thank you so much for these three photos — they are exactly the kind of images I was hoping
for. The page is updated and they are online now:

https://www.lesagedavid.fr/en/yishama

Each photo shows a clearly different surface, and that is precisely what I wanted to show my
readers. But I want to label them correctly, and here I need your help, because I am really
not an expert on steel.

**Question 1 — which steel is on which photo?**
Could you simply tell me, for each one: A Minor 17 → ? · F2 Astronaut → ? · G2 Hijaz 18 → ?
I would rather ask than guess and write something wrong on a page that carries your name.

**Question 2 — are there really three steels?**
On my page I currently describe three: nitrided, stainless and Ember steel. But when I look at
those three product pages on yishama.com, the steel appears as an option chosen at order time,
and only two are offered there: Stainless steel and Ember steel. Nitrided is not in the list.
So I am no longer sure what to tell people. Is nitrided still made? Is it only for certain
models? Is the choice always between two, or three?

**Question 3 — what does the steel actually change, for a player?**
This is the part I care about most. All I have understood so far is that some kinds of
nitriding make each note ring longer or shorter. That is a very thin understanding, and I do
not want to repeat it if it is wrong or incomplete. In two or three sentences, in your own
words: what does a player really hear and feel between one steel and another? Sustain?
Attack? Brightness? How it ages? Anything you say, I will write on the page as coming from
Yishama, and I will credit it.

My only goal here is clarity and honesty. This page is meant to teach, not to sell — I would
rather write three accurate lines than three impressive ones.

**One last thing:** do you think it makes sense to present these three instruments as a small
gallery of their own on the page, or would you rather see them used only as illustrations of
the steels? You know your instruments better than I do — I will follow your preference.
